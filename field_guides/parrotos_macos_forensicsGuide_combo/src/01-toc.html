0How to use this manual

This is an anchor document, not a course. It assumes you already know what a filesystem is, why you hash evidence, and roughly what you are looking for. What it gives you is the thing that is genuinely hard to hold in your head: which command, against which artifact, on which path, under which macOS version — plus the awkward Linux-side caveats that most macOS-centric guides skip because they assume you are sitting in front of a Mac.

You are not. You are in Parrot OS, which means every convenience Apple builds into log, plutil, mdls, codesign and fs_usage is unavailable to you. Roughly a third of this manual exists to route around that fact.

Reading order

Parts I–V are the conventional path and are ordered the way an examination actually runs: get the image mounted, understand the filesystem you are looking at, run the classical toolchain, build a timeline. Parts VI–VIII are the reference core — the artifact and persistence maps you will come back to repeatedly. Part IX is the interesting part and assumes everything before it.

If you are mid-case and just need a path or a command, skip to Appendix B (artifact paths), Appendix D (SQLite queries) or Appendix E (command card). The PDF has a navigable bookmark outline for exactly this reason.

Callout legend

▲ DEVIL

A hidden trap, a counter-intuitive behaviour, or an artifact that is easy to walk straight past. These mark the places where an examination quietly goes wrong — where a file looks deleted but isn't, where a timestamp lies, or where evidence lives somewhere no reasonable person would look.

■ CAUTION

Evidence integrity. Something here can modify, destroy or contaminate the image, or produce a finding you will not be able to defend. Read before running.

● NOTE

Context, version differences, or an explanation of why a thing behaves the way it does.

▸ TIP

A shortcut, a better tool, or a way to save an hour.

Command block conventions

Commands are shown at the Parrot prompt. A cyan $ is an unprivileged command; a magenta # means it needs root. Comments are dimmed, and output is shown in plain grey beneath the command.

# a comment explaining the intent
fsapfsinfo -o 0 evidence.dd
fsapfsinfo 20240508
Container information:
	Number of volumes: 5
losetup --read-only --find --show evidence.dd
/dev/loop0

Where a placeholder needs substituting it appears in angle brackets — <IMAGE>, <MOUNT>, <UUID>. Throughout the manual the following are assumed:

<IMAGE>your verified raw image, e.g. /evidence/mbp-001.dd <CASE>your working case directory, e.g. ~/cases/001 <MNT>a read-only mountpoint, e.g. /mnt/apfs DATAthe Data volume — where all user content lives on Ventura+ SYSthe System volume — sealed, read-only, signed by Apple

Version tagging

macOS moves paths around constantly. Anything version-sensitive carries a tag:

13+ Ventura and newer   11–12 Big Sur / Monterey   ≤10.15 Catalina and older   ALL stable across versions

■ CAUTION — SCOPE

Everything in this manual assumes you have lawful authority to examine the device in question: your own hardware, an engagement with written authorisation, or a lab exercise. The persistence and anti-forensics chapters describe how attackers hide so that you can find them. Applying them in the other direction, or examining a machine you have no right to touch, is not what this document is for.
